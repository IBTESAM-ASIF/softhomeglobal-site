Where we are not the answer

We are not the cheapest offshore provider. Published FTE rates from larger offshore firms start at $1,280 to $1,600 per FTE per month, which is below our floor of $1,500. If the only thing that matters is the lowest monthly number, take that one.

We are a fixed cost. A percentage vendor bills nothing in a month you collect nothing. We bill the same either way. If your volume is genuinely unpredictable, that risk transfer may be worth the higher long-run price.

We cannot be in the room. If the job includes covering the desk or walking a chart to a clinician, hire locally. We will say so on the call.

Why anybody outsources this at all

Denials stopped being exceptions. On first submission 11.81% of claims are denied — and about about 90% of those are paid in the end. The money is usually there. Getting it costs three rounds, 45 to 60 days each of insurer review at $57.23 per denied claim in administrative cost, which is why US hospitals spent nearly $18 billion on overturning denials in a single year.

None of that work is difficult. It is just relentless, and it is nobody’s job in a practice where the person who could do it is answering the phone.

Questions

Is a seat cheaper than hiring someone in the United States?

On the monthly invoice, yes, and by a wide margin. The median US wage for this occupation is $51,140 a year before benefits, and government data puts wages at only 69.9% of what an employer actually pays — so the real cost of that hire is nearer $73,162. What you give up is presence: your seat is not in the building and cannot cover the front desk.

Are you the cheapest offshore option?

No, and we would rather say so here than have you find out. Published offshore FTE rates start around $1,280 to $1,600 a month, below our floor. What you get for the difference is a named full-time person on United States hours rather than a shared or overnight resource, a published price rather than a quote, and a one month minimum.

Why not just pay a percentage of collections?

Because the bill grows exactly as your practice does. Published rates run 4% to 10% of collections, and 10% to 12% for complex specialties — which on $1,000,000 of collections is $40,000 to $100,000 a year. It also points the vendor at whatever is quickest to collect, which is rarely the ageing end of the report.

When is hiring locally genuinely the better answer?

When the role needs to be in the room — a practice manager, or a biller who also covers the front desk and makes judgement calls with a clinician standing over them. Offshoring a role whose value is presence is a false economy, and we will say so on the call rather than sell you a seat.

What is the catch with a flat seat?

You are buying capacity, not an outcome. If nobody on your side keeps the worklist stocked and tells us what "done" looks like, you will have paid for a full-time person to be underused. A percentage vendor carries that risk for you; we do not.

Why is this work worth outsourcing at all?

Because denials are now routine rather than exceptional — 11.81% of claims were denied on first submission — and roughly about 90% of them are eventually paid. The money is usually there. Somebody just has to spend three rounds, 45 to 60 days each getting it, at $57.23 per denied claim of admin cost each.
