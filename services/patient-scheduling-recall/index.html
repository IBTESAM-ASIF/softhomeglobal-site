Scheduling, Recall & No-Show Recovery

Scheduling, Recall & No-Show Recovery from Soft Home Global costs $1,500 per seat per month. That is one full time trained person, forty hours a week, working United States business hours inside your own system. One seat, one month, cancel any time.

An empty slot cannot be sold again, and every patient overdue for a follow-up, an annual or a procedure is work the practice has already won and never booked. When the front desk is busy with the people standing in front of it, the recall list stops being worked, cancellations are not backfilled, and no-shows get noticed at month end when the week is long gone. This seat runs the confirmation calls, the waitlist, the same-day no-show chase and the recall lists as a fixed daily routine rather than as spare-time work.

What the seat actually does

  • Calls tomorrow's and next week's schedule to confirm, on the numbers and contact consents recorded in your chart, and surfaces a cancellation days early instead of on the morning.
  • Offers a cancelled slot straight out of a live waitlist of patients who asked for an earlier date, so the hour is refilled rather than lost.
  • Chases no-shows the same day while the visit is still in the patient's mind, rebooks on that call, and leaves only the brief voicemail script you approved.
  • Works the recall and overdue lists by due month, follow-ups, annuals and procedures ordered but never booked, until each one is booked, declined or unreachable.
  • Logs why a patient will not rebook, cost, transport, moved away, now seeing someone else, so the list stops being called blind next month.
  • Reports a written weekly count: slots filled, recalls booked, no-shows recovered, and who could not be reached after the number of attempts you set.

What it costs

$1,500

Per seat, per month, in US dollars. Full time, forty hours a week, on US hours. Not per hour, not per claim, and never a share of your collections, because a percentage model makes your bill rise exactly when your month goes well and makes next year impossible to budget.

How a seat starts

You issue one named login. We sign the Business Associate Agreement before it is used. We agree the numbers you will judge the seat on, which you pick and you measure. Then we train for about a week on your workflow and the seat starts. Every Friday you get a written report of what was done, what the numbers were, and what went wrong.

Questions

How much does scheduling, recall & no-show recovery cost?

$1,500 per seat per month. That is a full time person, forty hours a week, working United States business hours. Not per hour, not per claim, and not a percentage of what you collect.

Is there a minimum contract?

One seat for one month, cancellable with thirty days notice. There is no annual lock-in.

Do we have to change systems?

No. The work happens inside whatever you already use, through a named login you issue. We do not sell software and we do not route your data through anything of ours.

How quickly can a seat start?

About a week from the agreement, which covers training on your workflow, your payer mix and your system. You are not charged extra for that week.

Will our patients realize they are speaking to someone overseas?

Yes, if they ask, and the person will say so. Nobody is given a false American name or a false location. The seat calls as your practice, on your hours, from a number you own and authorize us to display, trained on your provider names, your sites and your cancellation policy. The honest limit is the call itself: a hard-of-hearing patient, a heavy regional accent, or a patient who speaks a language the seat does not, is a harder call from an offshore desk than from your front desk. If much of your list is elderly or not English-speaking, tell us before you buy and we will say whether it is worth trying.

Does the seat book directly in our schedule, or hand us a list to type in?

Inside yours, on a named login you issue, with the permissions you set. It books, moves and cancels under rules written down first: which provider, which visit type, how far ahead, what may be double-booked and what never is, and what needs your approval before it goes in the schedule. If you would rather it hold no booking rights for the first few weeks, it prepares the booking and your front desk enters it. Three things must work on your side: remote access to the schedule, a phone line the seat can dial out on and patients can call back to, and a signed business associate agreement. Some payer and state Medicaid contracts restrict offshore access to patient data, so check yours first.

How does the seat know what a patient is due for? Isn't that a clinical judgment?

Your clinicians own it, and that is worth being blunt about. The seat is not a clinician and works only from what is already in the chart: the recall entry, the order, the due date, the interval your provider set. It does not decide that a patient needs a procedure, does not set or change a recall interval, and does not answer a clinical question on the phone. If a patient starts describing symptoms, the call goes to your staff the same day rather than being handled. Where nothing has been entered at all, the most the seat can do is prepare a list of patients with no future appointment for someone credentialed on your side to review and decide on.

We are a small practice. Does a full-time person for this actually make sense?

Sometimes not, and we would rather say so than sell you a seat that idles. A seat is one full-time person on US hours, $1,500 a month, paid up front, one month minimum, thirty days' notice. If your daily confirmation calls and your recall list together do not fill a working day, the seat only makes sense if it also carries something else, such as patient balance calls, eligibility checks or the inbound phone. Tell us your appointment volume and roughly how many patients are sitting overdue, and we will tell you whether that is a seat or half of one.

How do we know it is working? What no-show improvement do you promise?

None, in numbers. We do not know your no-show rate, and anyone quoting a figure before seeing your schedule is guessing. What we do is take a baseline in the first week, empty slots, no-shows and patients overdue, then report the same counts every week after, so the seat is measured against your own starting point rather than an industry claim. And a reminder call does not fix a patient with no transport or no money. It tells you that is the reason, which is worth knowing before you keep calling.
