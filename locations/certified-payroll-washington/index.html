Certified Payroll in Washington

Soft Home Global provides Davis-Bacon and state prevailing wage filing for Washington public works contractors at $1,600 per seat per month. Full time, worked during Washington business hours, inside your own system.

What is specific to Washington

Washington L&I requires a Statement of Intent before work starts and an Affidavit of Wages Paid before final payment.

A rejected filing that sits unworked is the same as no filing, and on a Washington public job it can hold up a progress payment on the whole contract. The seat works rejections until the state marks the record approved.

What the seat does

  • Weekly certified payroll for every Washington crew on every job
  • Fringe benefits and classifications checked before submission
  • Rejections worked until the state marks the record approved
  • Every submission visible to you before and after it goes

Most of the Washington work we see comes out of Seattle, Spokane and Tacoma, though nothing about a seat depends on where your office is — the queue is worked through your system, not from your building.

Washington borders 2 states, and practices near those lines almost always carry at least one out-of-state Medicaid enrollment. That is a separate portal, a separate filing window and a separate appeal route from Apple Health, and it is the queue that most often goes unworked because it belongs to nobody.

Working Washington hours

Washington runs on Pacific time, 13 hours behind our floor in Rawalpindi. To be at a desk for your nine to five, the shift here runs 10pm to 6am, and 9pm to 5am once the clocks go forward. Pakistan does not observe daylight saving, so the shift moves on this side rather than yours.

This is the part that decides whether follow-up actually happens. A payer’s phone lines are open during Washington business hours and nobody else’s, so a team working its own daylight can only ever leave messages and read portals. Ours is at a desk while yours is.

It is worth comparing against the two things it usually replaces: a percentage-of-collections vendor, which typically charges a share of everything it touches and so costs more every year your practice grows; and an in-house hire, where the salary is only part of the number once benefits, payroll tax, a desk and the cost of covering holiday are counted. Run your own figures rather than take ours.

No. It is a flat monthly rate per seat. A percentage model bills you most in your best month and makes next year impossible to budget, and it quietly points the vendor at the easy accounts rather than the ones sitting at 120 days.
